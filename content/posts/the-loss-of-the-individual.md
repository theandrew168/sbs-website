An opinion piece about modern social media trends?
Can't believe it took me this long, to be honest.
Anyone who knows me also knows about my strong opinions on the subject.

# Social Media (Race to the Bottom - Crudbump)

Do you ever find yourself looking at something like YouTube, Reddit, or HackerNews and jump from the title directly to the comments?
What's up with that?
Why are comments more interesting than the content itself?
People like to fit in with other people and comments are (or at least appear to be) a shortcut to consensus.
They replace individual thoughts and influence that you normally get from real discussions with real people.
Modern engagement preys on human psycology.
The "wow face" thumbnails, polarizing / worst-case news.
I have to remind myself that the comments are most likely written by bots anyhow (dead internet theory).

More and more these days, people feel closed off, isolated, anxious, and scared of each other.
Walls are up by default, especially when it comes to politics.
Blue vs Red didn't use to be this toxic, exteme, and polarized.
Loneliness is a big problem as a result.
Will everyone just be sad, angry, and anxious, isolated in their homes and hoping they can buy the fix?

Ben Folds describes this as a "disease that makes strangers" of friends.
Watching M83's "We Own the Sky" music video nearly brought me to tears.
Makes me miss the days before toxic algorithm feed tech.
Can this be fixed or is the cat out of the bag?
You can always to disconnect and "airgap" yourself from the machine.
But even then, you are only one step removed if the people you associate with are still gonna tell you about what they saw on Reddit that morning.
Maybe that's enough, though?
Or will there someday be whole "airgapped" communities that live off the "cyber grid"?

Due to family circumstances, I've been looking through a lot of old photos lately.
People were hanging out with each other: fully present.
No phones, no internet, no engagement-mining algorithms stealing everyone's focus and telling them what to think and how to feel.
Are we even individuals anymore?
I think that depends on how you measure "influence".
In the old days, influence was local (for better or for worse).
You were influenced by the people and situations physically close to you (friends and family).
Opinions were more organic but maybe less worldly.

Even this post is likely just more [rare original data](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IU4ByUbDKNc) for the machine.
Maybe the way I'm feeling isn't even genuine: it's just another bucket to slide people into.
The "skeptics", the "luddites", the "doomers".
I wonder what they'll try to sell to us?

# LLMs (Moatless in Chicago - Grieves Audiotree)

Code itself is commoditized.
LLMs blow smoke up your ass and lie about progress and value.
How do you actually measure what they've done for you?
Their own evaluations are biased and can't be trusted.
Asking for their opinions of value is a conflict of interest (like asking a car salesman if buying a car is a good idea).
Value needs to be proven by data or payment.
Thinking about Sara Chen testimonials: it's all fake!
It's all an illusion: just a facade of value.
Feels like systems are just worse now: slower and less reliable.

Software development is hard.
I like to think about it like trying to a reach a specific point in an N-dimensional space.
There are two parts to this: knowing _where_ you want to go and knowing _how_ to get there.
Both can be quite difficult but I think the former is more important to get right.
There are many branches paths through this N-dimensional space but only a few will get you to your destination.
Otherwise you'll just meander aimlessly: chasing mirages and wasting your time in an endless [ant mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant_mill).

Perceptions of iteration speed are altered.
Expectations of velocity increase.
But speed doesn't matter if it gets you to the wrong destination.
Given the vastness of the search space, precision is key.
Where does that precision come from?
Experience, craftsmanship, and [tacit knowledge](https://cekrem.github.io/posts/the-tacit-dimension/).
Maybe this section should be called "The lost of the Craftsman".
